    The rules of life

    1. 01A cell is born with 3 neighbors.
    2. 02It survives with 2 or 3.
    3. 03Otherwise, it fades away.
    Devised by John Horton Conway, 1970 B3 / S23

    How to use the board

    Edit cells

    • Draw adds the cells you drag across; Erase removes them. They work the same wherever the stroke starts.
    • Pan moves the view and never changes a cell.
    • Editing pauses a running simulation and leaves it paused; press Play to continue. Esc reverts the stroke you are making.

    Place a pattern

    • Choose one in the Pattern library, then click the board. On a touch screen, drag to position it and release to place it.
    • R rotates it and Esc cancels. You return to the tool you had selected.

    Move around

    • Zoom with the mouse wheel or trackpad, + / −, or by pinching.
    • Pan with the Pan tool, a right-, middle- or Shift-drag, or two fingers.
    • F or Fit: Board shows the whole board. Fit: Live cells zooms to the live cells with a small margin, using the board's own coordinates, so a shape split across an edge fits the whole width.
    • The readout shows the rendered size of one cell in CSS pixels.

    Undo and restart

    • Ctrl/⌘+Z undoes and Ctrl/⌘+Shift+Z redoes. Each stroke, placed pattern, Clear and Randomize is one step; the last 50 are kept. Undo also restores the generation count.
    • Restart from seed goes back to the setup you started running from, at generation 0, and stays paused. The seed is taken at your first Play or Step after you edit the board. Restart can itself be undone.

    Playback

    • Space plays or pauses, N or → steps one generation, C clears the board.
    • Randomize fills the board about a quarter full and restarts the generation count.

    The board

    The board is 256 × 160 cells and its edges wrap: a pattern that leaves one side comes back on the opposite side.
